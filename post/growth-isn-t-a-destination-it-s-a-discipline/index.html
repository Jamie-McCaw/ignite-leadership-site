Growth Isn’t a Destination, It’s a Discipline

By Adam McCutchen, May 19, 2025

This week, I want to sound off about growth.

Too often, I see people land a new role or finally snag that long-awaited promotion, and then something curious happens. They settle. There’s this unspoken mindset of “I’ve arrived.” They kick their feet up on the desk (figuratively, or maybe literally), basking in the glow of their success.

Now, don’t get me wrong, celebrate your wins! You earned that milestone. You climbed that mountain. But beware: when celebration lingers too long, it morphs into complacency. And complacency? That’s the gateway to mediocrity.

Let me share a moment from a leadership town hall at the end of a week-long conference. During the open Q&A, employees had the rare chance to ask executive leaders anything. There were thoughtful questions about staffing, budgets, and resources. And then came Susan.

Susan raised her hand and, once again, voiced her concern that promotions within the organization were rare and seemingly reserved for those with the right connections. Her question wasn’t inappropriate. In fact, on the surface, it sounded fair. But it was also the fourth time she’d asked it in the past year.

Here’s where the story shifts.

That same year, four employees were promoted. And yes, they knew people across the organization, but not because they played politics. It was because they had intentionally invested in their growth. They worked on their strengths, developed new skills, and accepted stretch assignments even when those tasks fell outside their job description or offered no extra perks. They chose discomfort over convenience. They chose growth.

Meanwhile, Susan had remained in the same role, doing the same tasks, in the same way, for seven years. When invited to try something new, her response was always the same: “That’s not in my job description.” And just like that, she guaranteed that tomorrow would look exactly like today.

Here’s the truth: growth isn’t something that happens to you, it’s something you pursue. Promotions and opportunities aren’t simply given to those who want them. They come to those who prepare for them.

So here’s your call to action:

  • What are you doing today that your future self will thank you for?
  • Find a course, take on a stretch project, seek feedback, mentor someone, or ask to be mentored. Growth doesn’t wait for permission.
  • Don’t just show up. Grow into the leader, teammate, and difference-maker you were meant to be.
